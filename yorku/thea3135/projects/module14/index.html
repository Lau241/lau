Module 014: Speaking and Presenting

An analysis on the TED talk 'Do Schools kill creativity?'.

THEA3135, Richard Lau



I analyzed the TED talk 'Do schools kill creativity?' by Sir Ken Robinson: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iG9CE55wbtY



Content

Robinson says that today’s schools often hold back creativity instead of encouraging it. In this ted talk, he advocated for equality in valuing the creative subjects as the same as skills such as reading and writing.

His main point was that kids are naturally creative, but schools often teach them to avoid mistakes, follow a single method, rather than teaching them to explore or experiment. In this TED Talk, Robinson uses stories and real-life examples to make his ideas easy to understand. For example, he notes that schools usually focus more on subjects like math and science, while the arts and other talents receive less attention.

Throughout the talk, he uses friendly and everyday language instead of complicated explanations. This makes the message feel more accessible and easy to follow. Overall, the main idea he communicates is that everyone is creative in different ways, and schools should help students develop their individual strengths rather than forcing the same curriculum on everyone.

Timing & Pacing

The pacing of the presentation is deliberate and effective. Robinson begins with humour and light observations to quickly build rapport with the audience. Early jokes help relax the audience and capture attention before introducing more serious ideas.

Key points are spaced throughout the talk, with short anecdotes acting as transitions between arguments. This pacing prevents cognitive overload and engagement through humour.

Style

The overall style of the presentation is conversational, humourous, and storytelling-based. Robinson speaks in a relaxed and natural way, which makes the talk feel more like a conversation than a lecture. His humour also helps make the topic more engaging and memorable.

This performative style keeps the audience attentive while also reinforcing the message that creativity and imagination are strengths to have in life.

Visual Design

One interesting choice I found his ted talk was that there were no slides or visual aids. Robinson relied entirely on his vocal style to retain engagement and storytell. Without other visuals, this kept the audience’s attention fully on the speaker and the ideas he is sharing.

Because there are no other visuals competing for attention, his facial expressions become much stronger as well. Overall, I think it works very well with his engaging storytelling-based, humourous style.

Technical Execution

This ted talk's sound quality is clear and strong. The camera is always focused on Robinson so viewers can see his facial expressions and gestures. The lighting and filming are professional. All of these elements do not take away from the talk, but support it.

Overall, the technical setup supports the talk effectively.
